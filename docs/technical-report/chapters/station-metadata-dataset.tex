Our fourth source of data is current station information and live availability from the Lyft General Bikeshare Feed Specification (GBFS) feed\footnote{\url{https://gbfs.lyft.com/gbfs/2.3/bkn/en/gbfs.json}}, ie.\@ the same feed used by the backend.
Two endpoints are merged: \texttt{station\_information} (static: name, location, capacity) and \texttt{station\_status} (live: bikes/docks available, operational flags).

The station metadata feed combines two GBFS endpoints into a single table used by the backend. The static endpoint provides the descriptive properties of each station, while the live endpoint reports the current availability and operational state.

\subsubsection{Original Structure}
The merged dataset is organized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{From \texttt{station\_information} (static):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Station ID}: a numeric GBFS identifier unique to the feed.
        \item \textbf{Short Name}: the public station identifier used in the Citi Bike trip data, which is the key needed for cross-dataset joins.
        \item \textbf{Name}: the full station name.
        \item \textbf{Latitude} and \textbf{Longitude}: the station coordinates.
        \item \textbf{Capacity}: the total number of docks at the station.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{From \texttt{station\_status} (live):}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Bikes available}: the current number of bikes that can be rented.
        \item \textbf{Docks available}: the current number of empty docks.
        \item \textbf{Operational flags}: \texttt{is\_installed}, \texttt{is\_renting}, and \texttt{is\_returning}, which indicate whether the station is active and usable.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Extracted Features}
The backend also derives an \texttt{active} indicator from \texttt{is\_installed}, \texttt{is\_renting}, and \texttt{is\_returning}. This feature is useful for filtering out inactive stations in live views and for avoiding stations that are temporarily unavailable.

\subsection{Analysis and Insights}

% state (live bikes/docks), and spatial distribution (map). Each perspective

The exploratory analysis of the station metadata is organised around three
questions: how station sizes are provisioned, how availability varies in
real-time, and how stations are distributed geographically. The capacity
distribution reveals whether the network relies on many small stations or a
smaller number of large hubs; this informs aggregation choices and marker
scaling in map views. The live availability metrics (bikes and docks available)
reveal temporal imbalance and saturation: a high fraction of empty or full
stations indicates a redistribution need and suggests using saturation-aware
color scales or alerts in the dashboard. Finally, plotting coordinates on a
city map exposes spatial clustering, gaps in coverage, and the relationship
between station capacity and surrounding infrastructure.

Figure~1 summarises the numeric distributions used in the analysis  : the
histogram of station capacities, and paired distributions for bikes and docks
available at query time. These plots are used to select sensible axis limits,
bin widths, and outlier-handling rules for the final figures.

The spatial view is equally important. Mapping station points with size or
color encoding for capacity or current availability reveals both macro-level
patterns (e.g. densification in central boroughs) and micro-level issues
(isolated large stations, small stations adjacent to each other). In the
analysis this map is used iteratively to validate joins with trip data,
identify coordinate errors, and decide on spatial binning strategies for
aggregate views.

Beyond static summarisation, the analysis also inspects temporal snapshots and
short time-series aggregated at the station level. These lightweight
time-series highlight rapid depletion or replenishment events and are useful
for designing live-updating widgets or simple predictive indicators in the
final dashboard.

\subsection{Data Quality}

%     filtered out of live views.

%   - Cross-dataset consistency issue: the numeric station_id (GBFS) differs from
%     the public short_name used in the Citi Bike trip files, so joins between

The main data-quality issues found in practice fall into three categories:
operational flags, missing attributes, and identifier consistency.

Operational flags: a portion of stations are reported in the feed but are
effectively unusable because one or more of \texttt{is\_installed},
\texttt{is\_renting}, or \texttt{is\_returning} are false. The analysis
quantifies these cases and shows how an \texttt{active} boolean (derived from
the three flags) reduces noise in live dashboards. For live monitoring the
recommended approach is to filter by \texttt{active==true} and surface a
count of inactive stations to the operator.

Missing attributes: a small number of records lack capacity or coordinate
values. Missing capacity prevents inclusion in capacity histograms or in size
encodings on the map, while missing coordinates render a station unusable for
spatial overlays. The analysis documents the frequency of these gaps and
applies conservative filters: stations without coordinates are excluded from
map layers, and stations without capacity are retained only for non-capacity
analyses or imputed when appropriate.

Identifier consistency: joins across datasets require care. The feed exposes a
numeric \texttt{station\_id} and a public-facing \texttt{short\_name}. Historical
trip data use the \texttt{short\_name}, so all cross-dataset joins in the
analysis are performed on \texttt{short\_name} after validating uniqueness and
normalising formatting. This mirrors the inconsistency discussed in the City
Bike chapter and motivates adding a small mapping table in the backend to
ensure stable joins over time.

Other considerations noted in the analysis include transient measurement error
during feed outages (which appear as sudden drops in reported availability),
clock skew across sources, and duplicate short names caused by legacy station
renames. Each of these issues is handled through clear preprocessing steps and
documented thresholds so that the visualization remains reliable.
