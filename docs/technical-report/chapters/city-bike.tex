\section{City Bike Dataset}
Our main source of data is the City Bike dataset, which contains information about bike trips in New York City.
These datasets are published on a monthly basis and can be accessed through the Citi Bike website\footnote{\url{https://www.citibikenyc.com/system-data}}.

The process involved in writing this report can be found in the Jupyter notebook in our repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/davide-dona/nyc-bike-data-visualization/blob/main/src/notebooks/analysis.ipynb}}.

\subsection{Dataset Structure}
The data is organized in multiple CSV files, each containing information about bike trips that ended in that month.
Each row in the dataset represents a single bike trip and includes various attributes that describe the trip.

\subsubsection{Original Structure}
The original dataset includes the following columns:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ride ID}: A unique identifier for each bike trip.
    \item \textbf{Rideable Type}: The type of bike used for the trip. Can be either "classic bike" or "electric bike".
    \item \textbf{Started At}: a timestamp indicating when the trip started.
    \item \textbf{Ended At}: a timestamp indicating when the trip ended.
    \item \textbf{Start Info}: Information about the station and location where the trip started, structured as:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item \textbf{Station Name}: The name of the starting station.
              \item \textbf{Station ID}: The identifier of the starting station.
              \item \textbf{Latitude}: The latitude coordinate of the starting station.
              \item \textbf{Longitude}: The longitude coordinate of the starting station.
          \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{End Info}: Information about the destination station, structured similarly to Start Info.
    \item \textbf{User Type}: The type of user: "member" or "casual".
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Extracted Features}
Additional per-trip features were derived to support the analysis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trip Duration}: The duration of the trip in seconds, calculated as $\text{Ended At} - \text{Started At}$.
    \item \textbf{Trip Distance}: The distance between the start and end stations, computed using the Haversine formula and multiplied by a circuity factor of 1.3 to better approximate real routes.
    \item \textbf{Day of the Week}: The weekday extracted from the end timestamp, which is used to partition the data since trips are published based on when they ended.
    \item \textbf{Hour of the Day}: The hour extracted from the end timestamp, for the same reason.
\end{itemize}

Beyond these per-trip features, two aggregated datasets are derived from the cleaned trips to support station- and route-level analysis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Station Popularity}: for each station and time period, the number of trips starting and ending at that station, obtained by grouping trips by station.
    \item \textbf{Trips Flow}: for each pair of stations and time period, the number of trips travelling between them, obtained by grouping trips by station pair.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Quality}
Various data quality issues were encountered during the analysis of the City Bike dataset.

\paragraph{Different Structure}
During the analysis, we noticed that older datasets have a different structure compared to newer ones.
Older datasets may miss columns introduced in newer versions, such as "Rideable Type" or station-related fields, while including deprecated attributes such as Gender, Birth Year, and Bike ID.

This inconsistency required adjustments in the preprocessing pipeline to ensure that all monthly files produced compatible features.

Several data quality issues were considered during preprocessing.

The trip records contain a small number of outliers in terms of duration. Extremely short trips are often associated with docking or rental issues, while very long trips typically correspond to users keeping a bike for an extended period before returning it rather than continuous riding. Since these records do not accurately represent typical cycling activity, they are removed using reasonable duration thresholds. Similarly, trips starting and ending at the same station are excluded from distance-based analyses, as they likely correspond to round trips or erroneous records and would otherwise bias the results.

The dataset also presents some inherent sources of bias. First, it only includes trips performed through the Citi Bike system and therefore is not representative of all bicycle traffic in New York City. Second, trip distances are estimated using the Haversine formula applied to the start and end station coordinates. As this computes the great-circle distance between the two stations rather than the actual route taken, the reported distances should be regarded as approximations.

Station identifiers require particular attention. Although each station is associated with both a station ID and a public short name, these identifiers are not always consistent across different datasets and years. Consequently, stations are uniquely identified using the normalized short name.
